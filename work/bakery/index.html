    How it goes

    Kate Mullane, baker

    Kate spent eleven years on other people's benches. Two in Castlemaine, then nine at a wholesale plant in Coburg that turned out four thousand rolls a night and never once smelled like bread.

    She left in 2019 to make less bread, not more. Second Rise is one deck oven, one spiral mixer, one bench, and a starter she has carried through four kitchens. A hundred and twenty loaves is the ceiling, and the ceiling is the point — past that you stop touching the dough and start managing it.

    We do not do coffee. There is a place four doors down that does it better, and a queue moves considerably faster when nobody is ordering a cortado.

    Tram 11 to stop 42, Normanby Avenue. We are the narrow shopfront with the steel roller door pushed half up and a rack visible through the glass.
    The queue
    It starts on the footpath around ten to six. Standing in it is a genuine part of the experience and we are not sorry about that.
    Parking
    Two-hour zone on Normanby. Do not block the milk bar's driveway. Tony will find you, and he is retired, so he has time.
    Closed
