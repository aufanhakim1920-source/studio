4–5 SEPTEMBER 2026 · ONE SESSION

Motion,
measured.

Seventeen short films made in one night, in the order they were made. They are not a portfolio — they are a record of learning something the hard way, and several of them are here precisely because they failed.

The measuring found what the eye could not: every video made without real screen-recording footage was frozen, up to 93% of frames identical to the one before. The footage had been doing the moving; the animation never was. Then the measuring itself turned out to be the problem — the film with the most genuine craft scored worst of everything, and a film containing no animation at all scored the highest number recorded.

The first thing made that night, and the one Aufan said was better than everything that came after it. A row of cards dealt across black, one of them orange. No reference, no gates, no measuring — just a hand-built move.

SHORT

A REPORT THAT MOVES

The sound density of sixteen reference reels, counted: 7.08 sounds per second, silent 0.3% of the time. Real numbers, animated. Also the first sign of a habit that took the rest of the night to see — when the content became information, I reached for a layout.

PORTFOLIO SHOWCASE

Browser windows floating in a dark room, project names catching the light. The type is real and readable; the windows are real captures. Its problem is the one Aufan named later: it is a portfolio, and a portfolio is a list, so it becomes rectangles with labels on them.

GATES, NOT RULES · 66s

The longest film of the night, narrated. Its subject is the thing that actually changed the work: a written rule changes nothing, so build a tool that blocks the build. The note said 22 broken pages. The tool that replaced it found 60.

Second cut of the same script, re-staged in violet. About parallel agents — five running at once when the work divides cleanly. The palette moved but the structure did not, which is its weakness.

A ROOM THAT BARELY MOVES

The restraint experiment — how little motion can carry a film. It also carried a real defect for hours: window chrome at 1.09 contrast against its own ground, invisible, and passing every check because I had never pointed the contrast tool at it.

THE DARK CUT · 55s

The third staging, in deep violet with the windows arranged in depth. Of the four, this is the one where the layout finally has a foreground and a background instead of a grid.

10

LEARNED

SIX SECONDS OF REAL CRAFT

A card that squashes and stretches, a waveform that draws itself, a fold into a button. The most genuine animation craft of anything made that night — and every gate scored it WORST, at 86% frozen. I spent five iterations trying to “fix” it toward things that were worse. It is the single best argument in this collection that a metric is not a verdict.

runtime 6s
Ten films, and everything after them is gone. Films 11–26 were pulled on 5 September — Aufan looked at the run and said so plainly. They are not archived, hidden or waiting: the numbers are retired and never reused. What is left is what he actually kept.

What the ten are evidence of. One: choosing the right object beats fixing the film later. Two: a measurement is not a verdict — LEARNED has the most real craft here and every gate ranked it last, at 86% frozen. Three: a written rule changes nothing; only a tool that refuses to let the work continue ever changed anything.

And the hardest one, which took sixteen deletions to learn: a full green board is not a film worth watching.
