4–5 SEPTEMBER 2026 · ONE SESSION

Motion,
measured.

Seventeen short films made in one night, in the order they were made. They are not a portfolio — they are a record of learning something the hard way, and several of them are here precisely because they failed.

The measuring found what the eye could not: every video made without real screen-recording footage was frozen, up to 93% of frames identical to the one before. The footage had been doing the moving; the animation never was. Then the measuring itself turned out to be the problem — the film with the most genuine craft scored worst of everything, and a film containing no animation at all scored the highest number recorded.

FLATTEN

NARRATED · 43s · THE FIRST WITH A VOICE

A rolling mill that flattens waveforms, and every number in it is real. Each film from this page goes through the mill and comes out with its dynamic range crushed — because I compressed all of them to make them louder, and it killed them. RESOLVE went in at 3.4 LU and came out at 1.6. The Apple reference sits at 8.1. It ends on the finding: GAIN, NOT COMPRESSION — 13 of 14 lost range, 2.4 LU each, 0 gained.

Then it took its own advice: this film was mastered with pure gain and no compressor, and reached target loudness while losing 0.1 LU of range. The old method would have halved it.

SLIDE

8s · ONE OBJECT · TWO WORDS

Built after four rejections and a measurement that named the fault exactly: the two films on this page Aufan called good carry ~0 words on screen and run 6.7–10 seconds; every one he rejected carries 13–46 words and runs 20–28. The break is datable — film 17, where words went from 4 to 47, and the subject quietly became my own debugging.

So: one object, one arc, two words. A slide falls into the gate, the lamp comes up, and the slide becomes the site — one continuous transformation, never a cut. Built to the rules in video-shotcraft, a repo that had been sitting unopened: one protagonist, no handheld shake, a single clipped light sweep, four sound cues with nothing repeated.

What this set is actually evidence of. Three things, in order of how much they cost to learn. One: choosing the right object beats fixing the film later — LINE hit zero frozen frames on its first render purely because a conveyor cannot hold still. Two: a measurement is not a verdict — LEARNED has the most real craft here and every gate ranked it last, while BOARD's numbers went green as the work broke in front of me. Three: a written rule changes nothing; only a tool that refuses to let the work continue ever changed anything.

One film from that night is deliberately not here — it scored the highest motion number ever measured and contained no animation whatsoever, just static cards cut on a beat. It was called bad, correctly, and it stays down.
