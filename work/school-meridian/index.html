III — who teaches it

Marion Teale has run this course since 2011.

Fifteen years in the merchant navy, mostly the Australia–Japan run, the last six as second mate on ships where the sextant lived in a drawer and nobody opened it. She started teaching because a delivery skipper she knew lost his electronics 300 miles off Norfolk Island and spent four days discovering he could not actually do the thing his certificate said he could do.

She is not romantic about any of it. On night one she will tell you that GPS is better than she is, that this is a slow and fussy backup, and that the only argument for learning it is that it is the one backup nobody can switch off. She starts on time, she marks hard, and she will not let you round a number because it looks nicer.

She sails a 9.4-metre sloop out of Williamstown and has crossed the Tasman eleven times. She has never said “lost art” in a classroom and would rather you didn’t either.

NOT INCLUDED

  • The Nautical Almanac 2027 — $42 at the counter on night one, or buy it anywhere.
  • A watch. A $30 digital is genuinely fine; you rate it on night three and keep the figure.
  • Getting yourself to Point Ormond on nights 6 and 12. There is no bus that works.
  • Parking in Carlton, metered until 20:30 — the single most common complaint about this course.

Prices include GST. There is no online option and no recordings. If you cannot be in the room on a Tuesday, this is not the course — and we would rather tell you now than take $780 and have you find out on night four.
