Snow that fell before there were people.

ABSTRACTREV 41

Kirkwood holds 41 kilometres of polar ice, cut into 2,412 cores and kept at fifty-five below. The deepest of them reached 3,270 metres and came up carrying air that last touched the sky 1.2 million years ago. Drag down the core to go back.

ACCESS PROCEDURE

Ice is spent when it is measured. Every request is assessed against what the section can still answer, and against who has asked for it before. Allow a 6-week lead time from lodgement to dispatch.

Access is by research request only. Name the investigator, the holding institution, the measurement to be made, and the minimum mass of ice that will answer it. Requests without a stated mass are returned unread.

The committee sits on the first Tuesday of each month. It weighs the request against the prior sampling record for that borehole. A section already cut for three isotope studies will not be cut for a fourth.

Cores are held in 1 metre sections. The cut plan states the depth interval, the quadrant of the round taken, and the archive half that is never released. You receive the plan before the saw is run.

Ice leaves Hobart at −55 °C in a logged container and must arrive without an excursion above −20 °C. A broken chain voids the section: it is recorded as consumed whether or not it was ever measured.

Unused ice comes home. Melted ice is written off against the borehole and the entry is public. The index you are reading is the sum of every one of those decisions since 1987.
